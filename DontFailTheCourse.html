Dont Fail the Course:
Release Date: April 2022
Technical Lead/C# programmer
Project description: The game features a dungeon crawler set in high school, where players progress through the 9th grade by completing tasks such as solving math problems or battling characters like Shakespeare.

Technical Lead experience: As technical lead, I guided inexperienced programmers, assigned tasks based on interests, and provided challenges to enhance their skills and problem-solving abilities.

Basic AI system: Developed a simple enemy AI with Patrol and Attack states, customizable for diverse and dynamic gameplay encounters.


Intro:

Our main gameplay loop was to have a dungeon crawler set in high school where the student (The player) is trying to complete the 9th grade by completing specific tasks to graduate. This can be from completing certain math equations to fighting Shakespeare until they have completed their school year. When designing the game to include more school years, we realized we needed more experience and time to scope many systems and abilities. Another thing we realized during preproduction was that the majority of the programmers didn't have any experience with programming or Unity. This led us to decide that I would be the best fit for the technical lead position, as I had previously worked on games in Unity and other game engines.
Role As Technical Lead:

As the technical lead for the project, one of my main goals was to make sure that all the programmers had the opportunity to learn new skills while also completing the important systems and mechanics of the game. I did this by initially inquiring the other programmers about what areas of the game they were most interested in working on. At the same time we had been breaking down the different systems that needed to be implemented for the game to function. Once finished I was able to divide all of the tasks as well as give some more challenging tasks. These challenges were meant to help push them to learn how to solve problems in a more programming mindset or how to properly read through the documentation or research the solution.


Throughout the production I was able to quickly learn how to better divide tasks between all of the programmers. As everyone was giving feedback on how challenging the tasks were or the speed the tasks were being completed I was able to better distribute different tasks. By the time we were releasing the game all of the programmers were able to not only learn new things when it came to programming in Unity but complete their tasks quicker and at a better rate than before.
Mechanics worked on:

The main area of the game I was in charge of was working on the enemy AI system. When I started designing the AI I wanted it to remain simple so I made 2 states, Patrol and Attack. I was then able to customize them by having the values that represented attacking, their speed, and their speed of attack in between a range. This allowed for the specific room enemies to be different from the other ones while also keeping each individual enemy having a slightly unique attack timer to keep the players on their toes.
